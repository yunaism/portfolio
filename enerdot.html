Forecasts that don't cheat

Data pipelines and evaluation for electricity-market forecasting, built so that a model only sees what would really have been available at forecast time.

My part: Airflow wrapper, availability probe, preprocessing, retraining gate and site mapping. The forecasting framework, including candidate model selection, already existed.

problem

Forecasts of electricity-market prices (SMP) and solar generation were built on KPX market data. Two things can make a forecast look better than it is: features that would not yet have been published at prediction time, and evaluation splits that let the model see the future.

what I did

  • Pipelines. Airflow/MySQL ETL for KPX electricity-market data in Bronze and Silver layers, grouping Bronze → Silver per dataset and separating ETL, inference and evaluation DAGs by their actual input dependencies.
  • Availability probe. A DAG that polled the KPX API every 30 minutes and logged five observed states (not available, partial, complete, rate-limited, error). This turned an undocumented publication time into measured data for scheduling.
  • Point-in-time features. Removed a demand feature that was not yet published at forecast time, and filtered out weather forecasts issued after the prediction cutoff.
  • Lag bug. A row-based shift(168) silently crossed missing timestamps. I reindexed all sources to a common hourly axis, added unit tests, and made retraining halt on gaps of 168 hours or more.
  • Retraining path. Weekly retraining with a train–validation gap gate (MAE ratio) and automatic fallback to the previous model, logging model-file SHA-256 hashes to MLflow.
  • Site mapping. Two ID systems shared the column name plant_id, so weather and solar sites were matched roughly 254 km apart. I replaced this with a haversine nearest-neighbour mapping across 24 solar sites, and fixed silent empty SQL queries caused by NaN/float IDs.
  • Data layer. Post-DDL schema verification against information_schema and a partition-maintenance DAG.
  • Documentation. 24 operational decision criteria, each with its verification source, for team reuse.

what I found

  • Early solar results were optimistic because of shuffled splits and a training period that overlapped the evaluation window. After re-splitting by time, the honest numbers became the baseline.
  • Solar: 6.70% nMAE-10 across 24 sites (≥90 kW) with validation-based per-site model selection (LightGBM, XGBoost, CatBoost, random forest, linear, pvlib), against 13.49% for a default-parameter pvlib physical baseline.
  • Jeju day-ahead SMP: MAE about 14% lower than a 7-day persistence baseline (14.52 → 12.53 KRW/kWh) on a two-month held-out test set.

limitations and open questions

  • The pvlib baseline uses library-default panel geometry and is not calibrated, so it is a weak physical reference.
  • For SMP, the best model on validation and the best on test were different, with a very small gap; the improvement is reported as approximate for that reason.
  • The gate threshold for retraining is a provisional heuristic. It has not been backtested.
  • A gap in the raw data was found by hand; range backfill and coverage auditing are not automated.
  • I designed a D+1 shadow-forecast ledger (predictions stored before actuals are published, evaluated afterwards). It is paused until it can be calibrated.
